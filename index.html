Leadership Coaching Simulation

Supporting Jane

📋 Scenario Overview

You are a department manager at a software company. Jane, one of your direct reports, moved from Senior Software Engineer to Engineering Manager three months ago.

Note: Jane asked for this meeting, which is unusual for her: she usually handles everything on her own.

👥 People and projects

Jane

  • Your direct report, an engineering manager
  • Moved up from Senior Software Engineer three months ago; this is her first manager role
  • Built Project X when she was an engineer

Johnson

  • A fresh graduate, hired three months ago
  • Brought in to take over Project X from Jane

Project X

  • A critical, high-stakes client project
  • Jane built it from scratch

🗓️ How This Meeting Works

  • This is one meeting with Jane. She speaks first; after that, you lead the conversation.
  • At some point Jane may need a moment. When that happens, just follow what appears on screen.
  • Set aside about 30 minutes to do this in one sitting, without breaks.
  • You decide when the meeting ends. When you are ready, close it as you would in real life, for example by thanking her and saying goodbye. The chat then ends.
  • If the chat stays open after you have said goodbye, click Submit activity.
  • You have one attempt.
  • Feedback on your conversations with Jane and David comes later, after the short survey that follows your conversation with David has closed.

🎓 Learning Objectives

▼

By completing this simulation, you will:

  • Practice building psychological safety through empathetic listening
  • Apply coaching techniques to help someone discover their own insights
  • Learn to resist the urge to give immediate solutions
  • Develop skills in facilitating reflection and self-discovery
  • Experience how trust affects the depth of conversation
  • Understand the difference between solving FOR someone vs. helping them solve
